## What motivates me: the problem I want to solve

<!-- seccion: el-problema-que-quiero -->

I want to work on problems that demand connecting data, analytics and artificial intelligence within a complete enterprise capability. I am especially interested in scenarios where it is not enough to build a model, a Power BI dashboard or an agent in isolation, because the real challenge consists of integrating it with the organization's processes, information, applications, decisions and responsibilities.

I seek to remain at the frontier between the data platform and artificial intelligence. That is where it is determined how data is captured and transformed, what meaning it keeps, who can use it, how it reaches an intelligent solution and what evidence allows observing its behavior once it enters operation. It is also where it is decided whether a capability can scale without losing security, traceability, quality or purpose, and how what an agent does with that data is controlled.

My interest is not limited to demonstrating that a technology works. I want to build systems capable of producing value in a sustained way. That implies understanding the problem before selecting the tool, designing an architecture that can evolve, incorporating evaluation mechanisms and ensuring that the solution can be adopted, governed and maintained by the organization.

The problem I want to solve can be expressed simply: how to turn reliable data and institutional knowledge into intelligent solutions capable of expanding an organization's capacity to decide and act, without losing control over its purpose, its risks or its results. It is the problem I have been working on since Vesting, in 2023, and the one I lead today at Fundación CTIC.

## The agentic architecture I can bring: ARKHÉ and the harnesses

<!-- seccion: arquitectura-agentica -->

I have designed from scratch an advanced agentic ecosystem, ARKHÉ, to turn generative models into a structured, efficient and governable work capability. The architecture does not depend on a single agent that receives large amounts of context and tries to solve everything. It distributes the work among specialized components, each with a defined function, sources, tools, limits and acceptance criteria. The 13 agents published in the showcase run inside it; the complete design is in the agents document, and here I summarize what an organization gets with it.

In this ecosystem, a harness is the execution structure that controls how an agent operates: what instructions it receives, what context it can use, what tools it has available, what format it must deliver, what validations it must pass and how it must respond to exceptions. The model brings interpretation and generation capability, but the harness turns that probabilistic capability into delimited behavior within a process. This distinction is fundamental: a model can produce plausible answers, but it does not by itself know the obligations of the process, the authorized provenance of the knowledge or the consequences of an action. The harness incorporates those conditions and reduces the room for improvisation, so that precision does not depend only on using a more advanced model, but on the quality with which the task is structured and its execution is controlled.

## How the work is split between code, retrieval and agents: the view from Industrial Engineering and Industrial Design

<!-- seccion: distribucion-del-trabajo-agentico -->

The architecture distributes responsibilities according to the nature of the work. Deterministic code takes care of the validations, transformations and rules that require exactness and reproducibility. Retrieval mechanisms supply authorized and relevant knowledge. Specialized agents interpret, synthesize, recommend or coordinate tools. Controls verify that each result meets the defined conditions before advancing to the next stage. That is how the 13 agents of the showcase work: none receives all the knowledge or all the tools.

The specialization of agents serves a function similar to the distribution of work within a production system. A component that tries to solve every stage accumulates instructions, context, tools and possibilities of error. When responsibilities are separated with clear criteria, each agent can operate on a narrower space, use less context and be evaluated through specific conditions.

This way of designing keeps a direct relationship with my training in Industrial Engineering. I analyze the ecosystem as a flow system: I identify inputs, activities, constraints, handoffs, accumulations, rework and results. My purpose is not to keep many agents busy, but to get the architecture to complete the work with the least possible complexity, consumption and repetition.

Industrial Design brings another essential dimension. People need to understand what function the solution serves, what information it uses, what the state of a task is and when their intervention is required. That is why the agentic architecture must also design the transitions between people and agents, avoiding both automatic trust and the need to indiscriminately review every result.

## Context as a limited resource, controls at several levels and knowing how to fail

<!-- seccion: contexto-controles-y-abstencion -->

I designed ARKHÉ to manage context as a limited and costly resource. Instead of transferring all available knowledge to each execution, the system identifies what information each component needs, retrieves only the relevant evidence and keeps summarized states when the complete history is not needed. That reduces noise, lowers unnecessary token consumption and improves the relationship between the information supplied and the task to be solved. Token economy is not pursued through indiscriminate cuts —a context that is too small is cheaper and at the same time degrades quality—, but by maximizing the usefulness of each unit of information: selective retrieval, specialized instructions, reuse of results, structured summaries and separation of the tasks that do not need generative capability.

Precision is built through controls at different levels. The agent must use the authorized sources, respect the expected format, recognize insufficient information, declare uncertainty and keep evidence about the elements used; when the result can be verified through code, rules or structured sources, the architecture does so before allowing it to continue. The ecosystem also knows how to fail in a controlled way: a mature agent is not the one that always produces an answer, but the one that distinguishes when it can complete a task, when it needs additional information, when it must try an alternative route and when it is appropriate to hand over responsibility to a person. Abstention and escalation are part of correct behavior.

Observability makes it possible to analyze the functioning of the whole and not only the final answer: which component intervened, what context it received, which tool it used, which validations it passed, how much the execution cost and where an exception occurred. This evidence allows comparing behaviors, identifying waste and improving the system without depending on later interpretations, as the Vesting platform did with up to 23 agents at once.

## Technical precision, business value and what I measured in 120 scenarios

<!-- seccion: precision-y-economia-de-tokens -->

I distinguish between technical precision and business value. An agent can answer correctly and still not improve the process. That is why the evaluation must observe whether it reduces times, avoids rework, expands capacity, facilitates decisions or improves the consistency of an activity. The ecosystem is not justified by its sophistication, but by its ability to turn a technical result into a verifiable improvement.

This architecture represents one of my main professional differentiators. It integrates my experience in processes, data engineering, semantic models, applications, agents and artificial intelligence governance. I do not design agents as independent features. I design ecosystems in which knowledge, models, tools, controls and people can work in a coordinated, efficient and responsible way.

When I present improvements in precision, consumption or efficiency, I distinguish between design principles and measured results. I do not claim that the architecture reaches the maximum possible nor do I use superlatives without a verifiable baseline: every quantitative result states the version compared, the scenarios evaluated, the metric used and the conditions of the test. As long as that evaluation does not exist, I describe the architectural capability and not a superiority not yet demonstrated.

With that rule, in 120 representative scenarios I compared the architecture with a generalist agent that received in every execution the totality of the instructions, the available context and the enabled tools. Selective retrieval, the specialization of the harnesses and the reuse of intermediate results reduced the average token consumption per execution by 52%, and the rate of compliance with the defined criteria went from 71% to 93%. These are my own calculations, on tasks of document analysis, evidence retrieval, generation of structured deliverables and validation of results, and they must be interpreted within that scope.

## What I expect from an organization

I am looking for an organization that considers data and artificial intelligence as enterprise capabilities and not only as tools or temporary projects. This implies recognizing that their value depends on the architecture, the quality of the information, adoption, evaluation and the clarity with which responsibilities are distributed.

I value environments where there is technological ambition accompanied by discipline. I want to work where it is possible to experiment, but where relevant decisions are also documented, results are measured and risks are managed proportionally. I am interested in teams that combine autonomy with responsibility. I want to be able to propose, question and lead, but also to work with specialists who bring different perspectives and demand rigor from my decisions. I likewise expect the position to have a clear relationship between responsibility, authority and economic conditions. A leadership position should not be defined only by the number of topics assigned, but by the real ability to prioritize, set standards, coordinate teams and answer for the results.

I am not necessarily looking for an organization that already has a mature artificial intelligence architecture. I can also contribute in contexts where that capability is just beginning, as at Vesting in 2023 and at Fundación CTIC in 2025. What I do consider indispensable is the willingness to build it with purpose, assign owners, learn from the evidence and prevent each initiative from becoming a technological exception.

## How I work during a selection process: a real problem and the first 90 days

<!-- seccion: como-trabajo-con-quien-contrata -->

I prefer a conversation about a real problem to a conversation limited to walking through my résumé. My track record provides context, but the best way to evaluate my capability is to observe how I understand a situation, what questions I ask, what risks I identify and how I structure a route of intervention.

With a problem and enough context, I can lay out what I would try to understand first, what information I would need, which hypotheses should be validated and what the initial intervention with reasonable lowest cost and risk would be. I can also explain what I would measure, what results would be observable in a first stage and what conditions would justify expanding, redesigning or stopping the initiative. It is exactly what I would do in my first 90 days on the job: understand the system, measure the gap and intervene where it costs least and teaches most.

I do not start by promising a complete solution before knowing the system. I prefer to clearly differentiate between facts, assumptions, hypotheses and pending decisions. A responsible proposal must be concrete enough to guide the work and open enough to incorporate the evidence that does not yet exist.

I also consider it important to talk about the operating model of the solution. It is not enough to ask what should be built. It is necessary to define who will be responsible, who will use the capability, what information will be authorized, how the result will be evaluated, what risks must be controlled and how the solution will be maintained after its implementation.
