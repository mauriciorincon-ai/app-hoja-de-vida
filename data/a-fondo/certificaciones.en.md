## The DP-600: the analytics platform that underpins my profile

<!-- seccion: el-dp-600 -->

I prepared for the DP-600 between July and November 2024 and earned the credential in December 2024. It is the Microsoft Certified: Fabric Analytics Engineer Associate certification, it is current and it represents the most important formal validation of my specialty in Microsoft Fabric, semantic models and Power BI.

I studied for it while working full time at Vesting and building there a data ecosystem on Microsoft Fabric to analyze and monitor artificial intelligence agents. Fabric had at the time been in general availability for less than a year: there were no mature courses or a consolidated community, and the documentation changed frequently. I was not studying a platform in the abstract; I was formalizing what I applied every day, connecting pipelines, storage, transformation, semantic models, Power BI and observability.

This certification validates the ability to design, build and administer end-to-end enterprise analytics solutions on Microsoft Fabric. Its scope covers data preparation and transformation, the administration of analytics assets such as lakehouses and warehouses, the implementation and optimization of semantic models, and the protection and governance of information. It also requires working with SQL, KQL and DAX to query, structure and analyze data. How Fabric is used from the inside —Direct Lake, RLS, OneLake, Power Query— is in its own document.

## The semantic model: where Industrial Engineering, Industrial Design and Power BI converge

<!-- seccion: dp-600-modelo-semantico -->

For me, the greatest value of the DP-600 lies in the depth it grants to the semantic model. Data does not generate decision-making capacity merely by being integrated into a platform. It needs a layer that organizes the business entities, preserves their relationships, centralizes the metrics and lets different analytical products use consistent definitions. That layer represents one of the points where my experience in Industrial Engineering and my specialty in Power BI converge.

Industrial Engineering lets me understand the processes, resources, constraints and decisions that must be represented. The DP-600 validates my ability to turn that understanding into an analytical architecture: prepare the information, organize it in warehouses and models, structure verifiable measures in DAX and make it available through business-oriented analysis experiences.

Industrial Design brings a complementary dimension. An analytical architecture can be technically correct and still fail if people do not understand how to use it. That is why my specialty in Power BI does not end with building the model. It includes the way information is presented, the hierarchy with which it is organized and the ease with which a person can travel through it from an executive view down to the evidence that supports the result. It is the reason why the Banco Pichincha dashboards were adopted by more than 50 users and those of Fundación CTIC are used by some 75.

## How I use the DP-600 today: from Microsoft Fabric at Vesting to Power BI at Fundación CTIC

<!-- seccion: dp-600-en-vesting-y-ctic -->

The overlap between studying and working at Vesting was especially significant. The platform I was building while I studied ended up operational, from scratch, on Microsoft Fabric: 12 clients integrated, 120 tables, 20 GB, 1,000 events per day and up to 23 agents monitored at a time, in real time. Each domain of the exam —ingestion, lakehouse, warehouse, semantic model, security— had an equivalent in something I was deploying that same week.

Currently, this certification also supports my work at Fundación CTIC. There I use Power BI to structure 42 analytical products aimed at administrative and clinical leaders, while my knowledge of Microsoft Fabric strengthens the architectural vision needed to integrate data, analytics and artificial intelligence under common principles of security, traceability and governance.

The DP-600 does not define my whole profile, but it does validate its analytical core. It proves that I can develop an end-to-end solution, from data preparation to the semantic model and the decision experience. On that base the intelligent applications, the agents and the enterprise artificial intelligence architectures are integrated afterwards. And it is the reason why, in addition to AI engineering and strategy roles, I am interested in a leadership role in BI and analytics: the DP-600 is the credential of that trade.

## The AI-103: developing artificial intelligence applications and agents on Azure

<!-- seccion: el-ai-103 -->

Since July 2026 I have been following the path for the AI-103 exam, oriented toward developing artificial intelligence applications and agents on Azure; to date I have completed 21 modules. I do not present this credential as earned. Until I pass the exam, it must appear in my profile as a certification in preparation and not be part of the count of certifications achieved.

The AI-103 leads to the Microsoft Certified: Azure AI Apps and Agents Developer Associate certification. Its focus is on the design, development and implementation of advanced artificial intelligence solutions using Python, Azure services and Microsoft Foundry (formerly Azure AI Foundry). The path covers generative artificial intelligence applications, agents, natural language processing, information extraction and solutions related to visual content. From this path, and from the IBM credentials, comes the NLP I declare among my skills.

This path takes over the training space previously occupied by the AI-102, but it should not be described as if both exams were equivalent. The AI-103 incorporates an explicit orientation toward generative applications and agents built on Microsoft Foundry, although it retains areas such as language, vision and information extraction. That is why I removed the AI-102 from my professional narrative —my two previous CVs listed it as "in progress" and I never earned it— and adopted the current path without attributing to myself a credential I have not earned.

## Why the AI-103 connects with Vesting and with the AI strategy at Fundación CTIC

<!-- seccion: ai-103-vesting-y-ctic -->

The AI-103 connects directly with my experience at Vesting. There I understood that developing an agent does not consist only of designing a prompt or connecting a model. It is necessary to define the purpose of the solution, structure the context it needs, select its sources, delimit the tools it can use, manage exceptions and establish when it must request human intervention. With that criterion the 27 agents in the Vesting inventory were built on an 11-stage core process.

It also relates to my current responsibility of leading an institutional artificial intelligence strategy at Fundación CTIC. To correctly evaluate a proposal it is not enough to know the principles of governance. It is necessary to understand how the applications are built, how they retrieve knowledge, how they use tools and which technical dependencies they introduce. That depth makes it possible to formulate applicable controls and avoid policies disconnected from the reality of development: the 7 use cases formally evaluated were judged with that builder's eye.

My training in Industrial Engineering provides the starting point: before designing an agent I need to understand the activity, its inputs, rules, exceptions and effects on the complete process. Industrial Design broadens that perspective toward the interaction between the person and the solution. An agent must not only work; it must properly communicate its capabilities, limits and needs for intervention.

The AI-103 completes the building dimension of my profile. The DP-600 organizes the data and the analytical capability. The AI-103 extends that base toward applications and agents capable of interpreting information, connecting knowledge and using tools within business processes.

## The AI-300: taking artificial intelligence to a reliable operation (MLOps and GenAIOps)

<!-- seccion: el-ai-300 -->

Since July 2026 I have also been following the path for the AI-300 exam, which completes the third component of my specialization; I have completed 10 modules. Until I have passed the exam, it must be presented as a certification under development and not as an earned credential.

The AI-300 leads to Microsoft Certified: Machine Learning Operations Engineer Associate. Its focus covers the infrastructure and practices needed to operationalize machine learning models and generative artificial intelligence solutions on Azure, integrating MLOps and GenAIOps within a common vision of artificial intelligence operations.

The path includes the design of infrastructure for MLOps, model lifecycle management, the operation of generative applications and agents, quality evaluation, observability, automation, cost control and performance optimization. It also incorporates Microsoft Foundry, Azure Machine Learning, GitHub Actions and infrastructure-as-code practices.

It takes the place of the DP-100: Microsoft retired that certification on June 1, 2026 and declared the AI-300 as its replacement. The change was not only one of code: the DP-100 was data science on Azure; the AI-300 is the operation of models and agents, which is exactly the stretch my career needed to formalize.

## Developing an AI solution and operating it are different capabilities: Vesting, Fundación CTIC and Industrial Engineering

<!-- seccion: ai-300-operar-no-es-construir -->

This certification is especially relevant to my career because it answers a difference I have learned in practice: developing an artificial intelligence solution and operating it reliably are different challenges. A demo can prove that a capability is technically possible. Operation also requires controlled versions, repeatable evaluations, monitoring, response to failures, traceability and criteria for updating or retiring the solution.

At Vesting I built the data platform needed to observe agents in production —up to 23 at a time, in real time— and structured a replicable process for developing new solutions. There I understood that observability must not be limited to confirming that a service is available. It must also make it possible to analyze latency, errors, utilization, costs, functional behavior and situations that require human review.

At Fundación CTIC, this perspective connects with the lifecycle established within the institutional artificial intelligence strategy. An initiative is not governed merely because it has passed an initial evaluation. It must remain observable, have owners and keep mechanisms for reviewing its behavior when the data, the models, the integrations or the institutional context change.

The AI-300 brings the operational discipline that makes it possible to materialize those principles in technical architectures. While UNE-ISO/IEC 42001:2025 structures the institutional management system, the AI-300 goes deeper into the technological mechanisms needed to deploy, evaluate, monitor and maintain artificial intelligence solutions. They are different and complementary levels: one organizes institutional governance and the other strengthens technical operation.

Industrial Engineering becomes relevant again in this dimension. Operating an artificial intelligence solution involves managing capacity, constraints, quality, variability, incidents and continuous improvement. Many principles used to stabilize a production process also apply to an intelligent system, even though its units of observation are executions, evaluations, versions and events instead of physical materials.

The AI-300 thus completes the transition from building a solution to turning it into a production capability. Its contribution to my profile is not only technical. It reinforces my ability to design intelligent systems that can be observed, evaluated, maintained and improved throughout their entire lifecycle.

## The IBM credentials: the data science base in Python, SQL and R

<!-- seccion: las-de-ibm -->

My training in data science was built through four IBM credentials, developed in two stages and around the main languages used for analysis: Python, SQL and R.

During 2022 I completed the IBM Data Science Professional Certificate (May – November), together with the Python for Data Science (June – September) and SQL for Data Science (September – November) paths. Those were the months I devoted to studying and getting certified between two jobs, and the investment was deliberate: I wanted to arrive at the next role with a formal base. This training strengthened my knowledge of data preparation and exploration, analytical programming, visualization, relational queries and model building using libraries such as Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, Seaborn, Folium and scikit-learn, with Jupyter and GitHub as the working environment.

Python brought the ability to turn an analytical problem into a reproducible flow. It let me move from exploring and preparing the data to training and evaluating models. SQL provided an equally important understanding: before building advanced algorithms, it is necessary to know how to locate, relate, filter and validate the information that represents the phenomenon being analyzed, with queries, subqueries and patterns on relational databases.

During 2024 (January – November) I complemented this base with Applied Data Science with R, in parallel with preparing for the DP-600. The path went deeper into statistical analysis, predictive modeling, hypothesis testing, SQL integrated with R, visualization with ggplot2 and building interactive experiences with R Shiny, with RStudio and Jupyter as environments and a final project with real data.

## What the IBM credentials left me: from data science to the TransMilenio and Banco Pichincha models

<!-- seccion: ibm-y-mis-dos-formaciones -->

Incorporating R did not answer to an intention to accumulate another language. Its value lay in strengthening statistical reasoning, the formulation of hypotheses and the discipline needed to differentiate between an observed pattern and a supported conclusion. Python gave me breadth to develop solutions; R reinforced the depth with which I had to evaluate the evidence. From there also come Shiny and the R dashboards I declare among my skills.

These credentials connect directly with Industrial Engineering. Process optimization, simulation, capacity analysis and prediction require understanding not only the tools, but the variables, assumptions and relationships that describe a system. Data science expanded my ability to observe those systems through evidence and explore behaviors that could not always be identified through descriptive analysis.

Industrial Design added a complementary responsibility: the results had to be made understandable. Visualization was not a decorative step at the end of the analysis, but a way of structuring information so that another person could interpret it, question it and use it. That combination of analysis, representation and experience is still present in my Power BI solutions and in the way I design interactions with intelligent applications.

The IBM credentials are not, therefore, an independent collection of courses. They represent the scientific and computational base on which I later built predictive models, analytics platforms and artificial intelligence solutions: on that base I trained with scikit-learn the demand-by-time-band models for TransMilenio and the churn, delinquency and risk models for Banco Pichincha, which reached production with more than 90% accuracy.

## How the certifications connect: an architecture of capabilities, not a collection of titles

<!-- seccion: como-se-conectan -->

Seen together, my five earned credentials and the two certification paths I am currently developing represent an architecture of capabilities and not an accumulation of titles. The DP-600 formalizes the analytical core of my profile; the four IBM credentials provide the data science base; and the AI-103 and AI-300 paths extend that trajectory toward the development and operation of applications, agents and enterprise artificial intelligence solutions.

The IBM credentials provide the data science base: understanding information, exploring it, formulating hypotheses, building models and evaluating results. The DP-600 organizes that capability within an enterprise analytics platform: it prepares and transforms data, administers analytics assets, structures semantic models and turns information into decision experiences through Power BI.

The AI-103 extends that platform toward applications and agents capable of using models, language, knowledge and tools to assist with or execute activities. The AI-300 completes the journey through the practices needed to deploy, evaluate, observe and maintain those solutions in production.

The sequence can be expressed simply: data science makes it possible to learn from information; the DP-600 makes it possible to turn it into a governed analytical capability; the AI-103 makes it possible to build solutions that use that knowledge; and the AI-300 makes it possible to operate them responsibly throughout their lifecycle.

This progression also reflects my career. I started by understanding processes through Industrial Engineering, learned to represent their data, developed models to anticipate certain behaviors, built analytics platforms to institutionalize that knowledge and moved toward agents capable of using information and tools within business processes.

Industrial Design runs through all these stages because it keeps present the person who must use the solution. Data needs meaning, visualizations need hierarchy, applications need an understandable interaction and agents must clearly communicate their capabilities and limits. A solution is not complete while its complexity prevents people from using it with judgment.

This combination defines my current profile. I do not specialize exclusively in data, Power BI, models or agents. I specialize in connecting these capabilities within enterprise architectures that make it possible to understand reality, make decisions and responsibly expand the organizations' capacity to act.

## The criterion by which I choose a certification

<!-- seccion: criterio-de-certificacion -->

I do not select a certification only because a technology is recent or has visibility in the market. It interests me when it formalizes a capability I am already applying, strengthens a necessary stretch of my professional architecture or forces me to go deeper into an aspect I need to master in order to take on responsibilities of greater scale.

That is why the DP-600 accompanied the building of a data platform on Microsoft Fabric. The AI-103 relates to my experience in designing applications and agents. The AI-300 goes deeper into the operation, evaluation and observability those solutions need in order to work in enterprise environments. Each path answers a concrete professional problem and not an isolated hunt for credentials.

A certification has value when it is contrasted with experience. The exam validates a structure of knowledge; the work demonstrates the ability to use it in real contexts, with constraints, risks, users and consequences. My purpose is to keep both dimensions connected.

Nor do I seek to get certified in every technology I come into contact with. I prefer to build a coherent and sufficiently deep sequence. A credential must close a stretch I am already traveling with my hands or prepare me for a responsibility that is clearly part of my professional evolution. It is also the reason why I stopped naming tools I studied back in the day and today neither use nor practice: a current credential and an honest list are worth more than a long list.

## The pace of learning as a professional capability

<!-- seccion: el-ritmo -->

The calendar of these credentials says something about how I learn. In 2022 I consolidated my base in Python, SQL and data science: three of the four IBM credentials, in seven months. In 2024 I expanded that training with R and prepared for the DP-600 in five months, while working full time and building solutions on Microsoft Fabric. Since July 2026 I have been continuing the AI-103 and AI-300 paths in parallel, consistent with my evolution toward building and operating AI applications and agents throughout their entire lifecycle. The complete timelines, with dates, are in the document on how I learn.

I do not consider that the pace of learning should be measured only by the number of credentials earned. It must also be observed in the ability to identify relevant knowledge, incorporate it in a structured way, apply it to real problems and turn it into practices, components and criteria other people can use.

My way of learning combines formal study, experimentation, documentation and professional application. I do not consider a concept mastered because I can explain it or pass an assessment. I need to check how it behaves against real data, business constraints, architectural decisions, operational risks and adoption needs.

I also understand that a technology certification does not represent finished knowledge. Platforms, services and architecture patterns evolve —the AI-102 itself and the DP-100 disappeared while I was studying—, and maintaining a professional competence requires reviewing those changes, renewing knowledge and continuously contrasting what was learned with practice. The formal validity of a credential is important, but real currency is shown in the ability to incorporate new possibilities without losing the principles of quality, security, traceability and business value that must remain.

This discipline also comes from my training as an engineer and designer. Industrial Engineering requires me to understand principles, relationships, constraints and mechanisms before intervening in a system. Industrial Design forces me to turn that knowledge into solutions that are applicable, understandable and usable by people. Certifications provide a formal structure, but professional competence appears when that structure can be transformed into a reliable capability within an organization.

What distinguishes my profile is not only the list of knowledge I have accumulated, but the ability to evolve it coherently. I learn quickly, but not in an improvised way. Each new capability is incorporated on top of previous foundations, contrasted with practice and integrated into an increasingly complete professional architecture.
