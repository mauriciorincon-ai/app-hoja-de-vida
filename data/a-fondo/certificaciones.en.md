**Five credentials earned** —the DP-600 and four from IBM— and **two tracks in progress**: right now I am getting certified in the AI-103 and the AI-300. Two codes that appear in my story and not in

## The DP-600: the analytics platform that underpins my profile

<!-- seccion: el-dp-600 -->

I prepared for the **DP-600** between July and November 2024 and earned the credential in
**December 2024**, while working full time and building a data ecosystem on **Microsoft Fabric**
at Vesting. Fabric had been in general availability for less than a year: I was not studying a
platform in the abstract, I was formalizing what I applied every day.

The certification validates designing, building and managing **end-to-end** analytical solutions
on Fabric: preparing and transforming data, managing **lakehouses** and **warehouses**,
implementing and optimizing **semantic models**, securing and governing information, and
querying with **SQL**, KQL and **DAX**.

Its greatest value, for me, is in the depth it gives to the semantic model: the layer that
organizes the business entities, preserves their relationships, centralizes the metrics and
makes different products use the same definitions. That is where industrial engineering
—understanding which processes, resources and decisions have to be represented— and the
**Power BI** specialty converge.

Today it underpins my work at Fundación CTIC: 42 analytical products for administrative and
clinical leaders, on an architecture that integrates data, analytics and AI with the same
principles of traceability and governance. How Fabric is used from the inside is in its own
document.

## The AI-103: developing artificial intelligence applications and agents

<!-- seccion: el-ai-103 -->

Since **July 2026** I have been following the track for the **AI-103** exam, which leads to
**Microsoft Certified: Azure AI Apps and Agents Developer Associate**: I am 21 modules in. I do
not present it as earned: until I pass the exam it is a certification **in progress**.

Its focus is the design, development and implementation of generative applications and agents
on Azure and **Microsoft Foundry** (formerly Azure AI Foundry): models, knowledge retrieval,
tools, evaluation and responsibility. It takes over the space of the AI-102, but it is not its
equivalent: it explicitly incorporates the generative and the agentic.

It connects with Vesting —where I learned that an agent is not a prompt wired to a model, but a
purpose, authorized sources, tools, limits and evidence— and with my responsibility at CTIC,
where evaluating an AI proposal requires knowing how it is built.

To prepare for this track I built the **Super guía AI-103**, one of the 13 agents in the
showcase: a coverage map against the official domains, with every piece of content tied to
current, dated documentation. It is my learning method turned into a public tool.

## The AI-300: taking artificial intelligence to a reliable operation

<!-- seccion: el-ai-300 -->

Since **July 2026** I have also been following the track for the **AI-300** exam, which leads to
**Microsoft Certified: Machine Learning Operations Engineer Associate**: I am 10 modules in, and
it is a certification **in progress**.

Its focus is operations: **MLOps** and **GenAIOps** on Azure —infrastructure for the model
lifecycle, deployment, evaluation, observability, automation and cost control of generative
applications and agents—. It is the third component of the specialization: the DP-600 organizes
the data, the AI-103 builds the solution, the AI-300 operates it.

It answers a difference I learned in practice: developing an AI solution and operating it
reliably are two distinct capabilities. At Vesting I built the platform that observed 23 agents
in production; at CTIC, the lifecycle is part of the management system. While UNE-ISO/IEC 42001
structures the institutional system, the AI-300 provides the technical mechanisms that make it
operable.

It takes the place of the **DP-100**: Microsoft retired that certification on June 1, 2026 and
declared the AI-300 as its replacement.

## The IBM credentials: the data science foundation

<!-- seccion: las-de-ibm -->

My data science foundation is **four IBM credentials**, in two stages and around the three
languages of analysis: **Python**, **SQL** and **R**.

In **2022** I completed the Data Science Professional Certificate, Python for Data Science and
SQL for Data Science, between May and November, in the months when I devoted the time between
two jobs to studying. Python gave me the reproducible flow of an analytical problem, from
exploration to training and evaluation with Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib and scikit-learn; SQL, the
ability to interrogate information where it lives.

In **2024** I complemented the foundation with Applied Data Science with R: statistical
analysis, predictive modeling, hypothesis testing and visualization with ggplot2. It was not
about accumulating another language: it was about strengthening statistical reasoning and the
discipline to tell an observed pattern from a demonstrable relationship.

On that foundation I later built the TransMilenio demand models and the churn, delinquency and
risk models at Banco Pichincha.

## How they connect

<!-- seccion: como-se-conectan -->

The five credentials earned and the two tracks in progress are an architecture of capabilities,
not a collection of titles. Data science makes it possible to learn from information; the DP-600
turns it into a governed analytical capability; the AI-103 builds solutions that use models,
language, knowledge and tools; the AI-300 operates them with evaluation and observability.

I choose a certification when it formalizes a capability I am already applying or closes a
stretch I am already traveling: the DP-600 accompanied Fabric at Vesting; the AI-103, the
agents; the AI-300, their operation. The exam validates a structure of knowledge; the work
demonstrates that I know how to use it. The pace at which I earned them, with dates, is in the
document on how I learn.
