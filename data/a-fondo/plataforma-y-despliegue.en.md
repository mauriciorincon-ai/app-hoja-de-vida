## My platform depth is in Microsoft

<!-- seccion: el-mundo-microsoft -->

My deepest platform experience lies in the Microsoft ecosystem and in Azure. I have worked with
Microsoft Fabric, Power BI, semantic models, lakehouses, warehouses, pipelines and capabilities
related to data integration, transformation, analysis, security and governance, and today with
Microsoft Foundry (formerly Azure AI Foundry) for generative applications and agents, which is
the ground of the AI-103 path I am taking.

I do not describe this experience as a list of services I have used. At Vesting, between August
2023 and January 2025, I designed from scratch a data ecosystem on Microsoft Fabric to integrate
the information generated by artificial intelligence agents, transform it, organize it and turn
it into an analytical capability for product and operations: the events of 27 agents that
served 12 clients, 120 tables, 20 GB and 1,000 events per day. The platform had to make it
possible to observe the behavior of the solutions, reconstruct executions and analyze volumes,
times, states, exceptions and consumption.

The DP-600 formally validates the analytical core of this experience, and I earned it within
that same role, in December 2024. The certification covers data preparation and enrichment, the
administration of analytical assets, the construction of lakehouses and warehouses, the
implementation of semantic models, and the security and maintenance of solutions on Microsoft
Fabric. What I did with each of those pieces —Direct Lake, RLS, Power Query, DAX Studio, Tabular
Editor— is developed in the Fabric document.

## Designing the complete data path at Vesting

<!-- seccion: el-recorrido-completo -->

That responsibility required designing the complete path of the data. I had to establish how
the events came in, what information needed to be preserved, which transformations could be
shared, how the main entities were represented and in what way the results reached the semantic
models and Power BI.

The architecture also had to sustain evolution. Each new integration could introduce different
structures, needs and behaviors. The challenge was not to solve each case in an artisanal way,
but to define a common base capable of accepting variation without losing traceability, quality
or analytical capacity. In practice that meant a reception layer that absorbed the shape of each
integration, a common representation of sessions, requests, responses, times, states and cost
from the preparation stage onward, and separate workspaces per client so that the separation of
information was architectural and not a discipline of whoever was querying.

My specialty in Power BI is part of that architecture and does not constitute an independent
layer. I work from the preparation of the information and the organization of the model to the
measures, the optimization and the experience through which a person uses the result. The
dashboard is visible; the value depends on the whole system that sustains it. I closed the
Vesting stage by leaving the ecosystem and the process documented.

This depth also connects with my training in Industrial Engineering. I analyze the platform as
an information production system: I observe inputs, transformations, constraints, controls,
accumulations, exceptions and consumers. I do not try to optimize each component in isolation,
but to ensure that the complete path produces reliable information at the right moment.

Industrial Design brings in the dimension of use. A platform can operate correctly and still
produce little value if people do not understand its results or find too much friction in using
them. That is why architecture, semantics and experience must be designed as parts of a single
capability.

## What I deploy and maintain directly

<!-- seccion: lo-que-despliego -->

In addition to my experience on enterprise platforms, I maintain a portfolio of public
applications built and deployed directly by me. This practice forces me to take on the complete
cycle: definition, architecture, development, testing, integration, publication, observation and
evolution.

Currently, the portfolio gathers six sister applications —Velo, Dash Agent AI, Probeta DS,
Hablemos San, Innmobiliaria and Nutri-Kids— plus CV Viva, this site. All are in sustained
operation: if they are on the site it is because they reached MVP condition, and they remain in
permanent evolution; Hablemos San, sealed in August 2026, is the most advanced. Not all serve
the same purpose or use the same architecture, but they share criteria on documentation,
testing, accessibility, performance, traceability and change control, and they share the same
toolchain:

- Git and GitHub as the source of truth, with conventional commits, branches per sprint and
  architecture decisions recorded in ADRs;
- continuous integration and deployment (CI/CD) with GitHub Actions: unit, integration and
  end-to-end tests, accessibility with axe, a performance budget with Lighthouse and a secrets
  sweep with gitleaks that blocks publication if anything fails;
- deployment on Vercel, with a preview for every change and production from the main branch;
  Innmobiliaria is served from the edge on Cloudflare Workers, and Dash Agent AI is not deployed
  because it lives entirely on the machine of whoever uses it;
- observability with Sentry and structured logging with Pino, always with metadata and never
  with user content, to know what failed and where.

Each application also publishes a sheet with its figures and the source of each one —measured,
calculated, declared or estimated—, because an app that demands provenance of every number on
its screen cannot publish loose figures about itself.

## Continuous integration as a quality mechanism: tests, accessibility and performance

<!-- seccion: ci-como-calidad -->

Continuous integration works as a quality mechanism and not only as a deployment automation.
Before publishing, the solution must pass the controls defined for its code, behavior and
artifacts. When a validation fails, publication must stop instead of passing the problem on to
the user.

The tests include different levels depending on the nature of each product. The figures, taken
from the published sheets of each app in 2026:

| App | Unit and integration tests | End-to-end tests | Line coverage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Velo | 740 | 153 | 96% |
| Dash Agent AI | 693 | — | 97.5% |
| Probeta DS | 267 | 24 | 90.7% |
| Hablemos San | 261 | 169 | 94% |
| Innmobiliaria | 172 | 76 | 98.3% of the engine |
| Nutri-Kids | 214 | 94 | 99.5% of the engine |

These figures must be kept in sync with the repositories, because their value depends on their
being observable and verifiable. Some of those tests are permanent by design: Dash Agent AI has
one that fails if the runtime opens an outbound socket; Velo, one that intercepts every browser
request and fails if a single one carries user data; Hablemos San, one that verifies that after
playing no trace of the child's voice remains in any storage.

I do not use the number of tests as a substitute for quality. An extensive suite can offer
little protection if it verifies irrelevant details or never demonstrates that it can detect a
real failure. That is why each control must be related to a concrete condition and, when it is
added, it must be observed failing against the scenario it intends to protect. That rule —a gate
is demonstrated by failing— belongs to the pipeline and is explained in its document.

I also apply performance budgets and accessibility criteria. A feature is not considered
finished only because it produces the expected result. It must do so with a reasonable
experience, preserve understandable navigation and prevent the product's evolution from
silently deteriorating its conditions of use.

## Answering for public products that can fail

<!-- seccion: responder-por-lo-publico -->

These deployments have real consequences. An incorrect modification can break a public
experience, affect a flow or produce inconsistent information. That responsibility forces me to
design mechanisms for prevention, detection, rollback and learning without depending on passing
the problem to an external infrastructure team.

Prevention is the CI that blocks; detection is Sentry and structured logging; rollback is the
per-change preview on Vercel and the possibility of going back to the previous version of the
main branch; learning is the summary of each sprint and the ADR of each decision, which remain
in the repository. When an app runs in the user's browser —Velo with Web Workers and Web Crypto,
Probeta DS with Python in WebAssembly— rollback is even more important, because there is no
server to switch off.

This practice strengthens my judgment for enterprise environments. An architecture does not end
when the code works in development. It needs a publication path, verifiable conditions,
visibility into failures and a controlled way to evolve. What I learned operating six
applications and this site is the same thing I demanded at Vesting of every new integration,
with the difference that here there is no one else to call when something breaks.

## Operating artificial intelligence demands an additional discipline

<!-- seccion: desplegar-y-operar-ia -->

Deploying applications and operating artificial intelligence solutions share foundations such as
version control, automation, testing, observability and responsiveness. However, an intelligent
solution incorporates an additional dimension: its behavior depends on data, models, context,
tools and probabilistic components that can evolve independently.

A conventional application can be validated through inputs and expected results in sufficiently
defined scenarios. A model or an agent also needs functional checks, but its evaluation must
consider variability, relevance, grounding, tool usage, consumption, latency and behavior in the
face of incomplete or contradictory information.

For this reason I distinguish between deploying an application and operating an artificial
intelligence capability. The second requires knowing not only whether the service is available,
but whether it continues to fulfill its purpose, whether it correctly uses the authorized
sources, whether it keeps its limits and whether the cost of producing a result remains
proportional to the value generated.

My experience already covers a good part of this cycle. I have built pipelines and analytical
models, developed predictive models in banking and in transport, designed at Vesting the
platform that observed up to 23 agents at a time in production, maintained public applications
and created ARKHÉ, my own ecosystem of harnesses, sources, tools, validators and retrieval
mechanisms. In three of the apps —Probeta DS, Nutri-Kids and CV Viva— generative AI runs with
guardrails, a declared budget and a deterministic fallback if the provider fails or the response
does not pass verification.

## Going to production is the beginning of validation

<!-- seccion: produccion-es-el-comienzo -->

I have also learned that going to production does not constitute the end of development. It is
the beginning of validation against reality. When a solution starts receiving different inputs,
interacting with users and using external services, conditions appear that no controlled
demonstration can fully represent. At Vesting I saw it in the events: an agent that behaved
well in tests began to accumulate retries with a specific integration, and only the per-session
telemetry made it possible to see it before a user reported it.

Observability makes it possible to turn those conditions into evidence. A solution must
preserve enough information to reconstruct executions, identify deviations, compare versions,
analyze costs and understand when it needs adjustments, restrictions or human intervention. In
my apps that information travels as metadata through Pino and Sentry; at Vesting it traveled as
events to Microsoft Fabric and from there to Power BI.

The AI-300 path —Machine Learning Operations Engineer Associate, in progress— formally deepens
this dimension through MLOps and GenAIOps. Its relationship to my trajectory is direct: it
strengthens the automation, evaluation, observability and operation of capabilities I have
already built from practice. It does not represent a change of direction, but the natural
extension of a professional architecture aimed at taking intelligent solutions from
construction to reliable operation.

production with Docker and Kubernetes are not in my professional experience. My world is

I have not operated a cluster or deployed a service on one. I do not claim real use of BigQuery
or Vertex AI: they are on my map as exploration, not as capability.

Of MLOps I have one half and not the other, and it is worth saying which. The half of taking
models to production and sustaining them I have done: predictive models in production at Banco
Pichincha and at TransMilenio, the real-time monitoring of AI agents at Vesting, and six sister
applications and this site with continuous integration, controls that block publication and
automatic deployment, operated by me. The half of packaging and orchestrating with containers,
no. My deployments are without a server of my own —Vercel, Cloudflare Workers, the user's
browser— and that is why they have never needed a container.

I prefer to say it like this, up front, rather than hide it in a list of tools. A résumé that
names thirty technologies does not distinguish the five it masters from the twenty-five it has
seen, and whoever interviews finds out in ten minutes. For the same reason I removed from my
skills tools I used at the time and do not practice today: a short list that is true is better.

## Architectural principles that transcend the platform

<!-- seccion: principios-transferibles -->

My technological depth is concentrated in Microsoft, but my architecture capability does not
depend only on the names of its services. The principles with which I design platforms remain
in force when the vendor changes: separation of responsibilities, preservation of lineage, data
quality, consumption-oriented modeling, least privilege, observability, change control and
lifecycle governance.

The need to select each component according to the problem also remains. The nature of the
data, latency, granularity, concurrency, cost, security and the expected consumers continue to
determine the architecture, even if the available services and their configuration mechanisms
are different.

I do not move a solution between clouds through a superficial equivalence of products. I move
the architecture questions and rebuild the answer using the ecosystem's own capabilities.
BigQuery is not simply another name for a Microsoft Fabric lakehouse or warehouse, and a Google
Cloud agent platform is not a nominal substitute for Microsoft Foundry.

Experience allows me to differentiate between the principle that must be preserved and the
implementation that needs to be learned. The data continues to need an identity and a
provenance. The model continues to need evaluation. The agent continues to require limits and
authorized tools. The platform continues to need observability and cost control. What changes is
the concrete way of materializing those responsibilities.

Industrial Engineering provides this capacity for abstraction. Before concentrating on the
tool, I analyze inputs, transformations, constraints, capacities, controls and results. This
structure makes it possible to understand a new platform from its function within the system
and not exclusively from the documentation of its services. Industrial Design also remains
relevant when the technology changes: every architecture ends up being used by people, and the
complexity of the ecosystem needs to be turned into understandable products, interfaces and
experiences.

This capacity to carry principles over without simplifying the vendor's particularities is the
basis of my multicloud evolution. It does not start from declaring mastery over all platforms,
but from applying a consistent architectural methodology and demonstrating it through built
solutions.

## Why that gap is smaller than it looks, and how I cover it

<!-- seccion: como-la-cubro -->

Two reasons. The first is one of equivalence: a lake on distributed storage, a columnar
analytical warehouse, a pipeline orchestrator and a semantic layer exist in all three clouds
under different names. What I did with lakehouse and warehouse on OneLake has its counterpart
in BigQuery, and what I do with Microsoft Foundry has it in Vertex AI; what does not translate on
its own are identity, costs and operation, and that is learned by operating.

The second is one of method, and it has dates. I learned Fabric from scratch at Vesting starting
in August 2023, with the platform less than a year past general availability, and I earned the
DP-600 in December 2024 after five months of study. Next.js and the application pipeline I took
up from July 2026, and with them technologies I had not used —WebAssembly, local processing in
the browser, edge deployment—, and today they are six sister applications and this site, built
and published. The AI-103 and AI-300 paths I have been taking since July 2026. And the last
thing I learned from scratch, with no certificate involved, was Codex, Antigravity and Claude
Code: I took them up from their release and it took me around a month to work with them
fluently. The document "How I learn" details the method.

Google Cloud is today a declared exploration, not a capability: in my pipeline there is a piece
called "Autonomous agent with Gemini and Vertex AI", conceived as the multicloud complement to
my certification path in Azure. It is in exploration with that word on purpose and with no
committed horizon; when it is built, it will be evidence, and until then I do not count it.

## My expansion toward Google Cloud: a declared exploration

<!-- seccion: expansion-google-cloud -->

I have made Google Cloud a concrete priority within my build pipeline, with a caveat I keep in
writing: it is an exploration with no committed date. My purpose is to extend an architectural
depth already consolidated in Microsoft through a verifiable solution that integrates data,
artificial intelligence, agents, observability, security and cost control within the Google
ecosystem.

The priority piece will connect BigQuery with Google Cloud's generative artificial intelligence
and agent capabilities, with Gemini and Vertex AI. It will not be a superficial reproduction of
an existing architecture nor a demonstration limited to asking a model questions. It will be an
end-to-end solution designed from the platform's own principles and services.

The architecture will have to integrate open or synthetic information, analytical processing,
controlled access to structured data, knowledge retrieval and tool usage. The agent will have a
delimited purpose, input and output contracts, acceptance criteria and conditions under which it
must abstain or request intervention, which is exactly what I demand today of the 13 agents
published in my showcase.

BigQuery will make it possible to structure the data and analytics dimension. Google's
artificial intelligence platform will provide the models, development mechanisms and
capabilities needed to build and operate the solution. The integration between both components
will have to preserve enough traceability to reconstruct what information was queried, which
tools intervened and what result each execution produced.

## What the Google Cloud piece will have when it exists

<!-- seccion: la-pieza-de-google-cloud -->

The first version will have a deliberately contained scope. My goal is not to design an
extensive platform that remains indefinitely open, but to build a finished, deployed,
documented and evaluable piece that demonstrates the complete path, with the same bar as the six
applications published in 2026: tests with Vitest and Playwright, accessibility, a performance
budget and a sheet with figures that declare their source.

The solution will incorporate observability from the start. Each execution will have to produce
evidence on times, errors, tools used, consumption and results. Cost will not appear as a later
consequence, but as an architecture variable that must be measured and managed, as I already do
with the declared budget of this site's chat.

Identity and permissions will also be part of the build. Each component will have access only to
the resources needed for its responsibility, and sensitive configurations will remain separate
from the code and from public assets, with the same gitleaks secrets sweep that runs in every
one of my repositories.

The piece will be considered complete when it can be deployed reproducibly, operate under
explicit controls, pass normal and exception scenarios, preserve evidence of its executions and
communicate precisely both its capabilities and its limits.

This expansion does not seek to replace my specialty in Microsoft. It seeks to demonstrate that
I can carry my architectural judgment over to a second ecosystem, learn its particularities from
practice and widen the range of platforms on which I can build enterprise data and artificial
intelligence solutions.

## A multicloud vision with depth, not superficiality

<!-- seccion: direccion-multinube -->

My main specialty will continue to be in Microsoft, where I concentrate the greatest
professional depth, the DP-600 certification and the experience of having built at Vesting a
data platform for agents from scratch. The incorporation of Google Cloud does not intend to
dilute that specialty, but to complement it.

I do not understand a multicloud strategy as the obligation to reproduce every component on two
vendors nor as an extensive list of known services. I understand it as the capacity to design
architectures that recognize which assets must remain under the organization's control, which
dependencies can be replaced and which capabilities are best placed on each platform.

The data, the business definitions, the evaluation criteria and the institutional
responsibilities should not be confused with a particular service. The models, the tools and the
managed capabilities can evolve as long as the architecture preserves enough separation between
the organization's own knowledge and the external technology used to process it. It is the same
reason why this site's chat and the Nutri-Kids assistant switch model provider through an
environment variable: the corpus, the guardrails and the tests are mine; the model is
replaceable.

This principle also applies to agents. Their purpose, sources, tools, limits and evaluation
criteria must be understandable independently of the generative provider. A change of model can
modify behavior, cost or latency, but it should not silently redefine the responsibility of the
solution.

Working with a second platform also strengthens the decisions within the first. Comparing
approaches forces one to distinguish which part of a solution responds to an architectural
principle and which exists only because of the convenience of a given service. That comparison
produces better decisions and reduces conceptual dependence.

My multicloud direction therefore has a clear criterion: proven depth in Microsoft and
verifiable expansion toward Google Cloud. I do not seek to present myself as a generalist of
multiple vendors. I seek to develop the capacity to design, converse and decide with judgment in
architectures where different platforms can participate without fragmenting the data, the
governance or the responsibility.
