## Why I chose to study Industrial Engineering at university, and the training in design

<!-- seccion: por-que-industrial -->

I chose **Industrial Engineering** because I wanted to understand how organizations work as
complete systems and, above all, how they could work better. I was interested in a discipline
that connected processes, people, information, technology and decisions, instead of observing one
isolated part.

I studied it at the **Pontificia Universidad Javeriana between 2009 and 2016**, with an emphasis
in Data Analytics Intelligence. That emphasis was the turning point: I discovered that data
expanded engineering's capacity to understand and transform systems, and that is where I learned
SQL, Python and Power BI. In the middle of the degree I spent a year in Melbourne, between 2013
and 2014, in an intensive English course that closed with the IELTS.

In parallel I studied **Industrial Design** at the same university, between 2011 and 2016, with a
focus on sustainability and cultural impact. From design I keep one idea that I apply daily: a
solution that people do not understand or cannot use with confidence is not finished yet.
Functionality is not enough; the solution has to communicate its purpose and reduce the
complexity it hands to whoever uses it.

All three things still define the profile. Engineering lets me structure complexity. Data lets me
observe it and explain it with evidence. Design forces me to turn that knowledge into something
someone can use to decide.

Industrial Engineering gave me the habit of looking for the cause in the relationship between
components, not in the component that fails. Ten years later I still start the same way: before
proposing a model, I draw the process and locate where the flow is lost.

## The first job was about processes, not data

<!-- seccion: el-primer-trabajo -->

My first job, at **Inglopres from August 2016**, was as a Process Engineer: a heavy machinery
rental and sales operation, an **ERP** (Odoo) that had to be implemented to integrate areas that
worked in isolation, and the ISO 9001:2015 standard as the framework.

The breaking point came when I tried to measure. I had been asked to improve the operation and I
discovered that half of the information did not exist: it was not captured, or it lived in
records that nobody cross-referenced. To evaluate a process I needed indicators, and to have
indicators I first had to build the data: define information structures, organize the
operational events and design SQLite databases that related them.

It was not a career decision. It was what the problem demanded. And out of it also came a
combination I keep: freedom to try alternatives, and a mechanism to evaluate, control and
reproduce the one that proves its value.

The detail of that year —the ERP, the work study, the team of twelve— is in the Inglopres
document. What matters here is the turn: my career oriented itself toward data because a process
cannot be improved without measuring it, and it cannot be measured without building its
information.

## From the process to the indicator, to the platform, to AI

<!-- seccion: del-proceso-a-la-ia -->

**From the process to the indicator** (Ceinfes and C&M Consorcio). I stopped improving one
activity and moved on to directing and supervising entire operations through their data: KPIs by
area, performance dashboards, a history so that each analysis would not start from zero.

**From the indicator to the platform** (Cafam, C&M Consultores and Banco Pichincha). Data stopped
being a report and became a system: the implementation of a WMS, an **ETL** pipeline and
dashboards that cross-referenced heterogeneous sources, and in banking the hardest lesson of BI: a
dashboard nobody uses is not a deliverable, and adoption is measured.

**From the platform to AI** (Vesting and Fundación CTIC). At Vesting I built from scratch, in
Microsoft Fabric, the platform used to monitor AI agents in production, and documented the
replicable process for designing them. At CTIC I lead the institutional AI strategy under ISO/IEC
42001, in the most demanding sector in privacy and traceability I have worked in.

Each leap has its own document with the detail and the figures; this is the map.

## The thread

<!-- seccion: el-hilo -->

The thread that ties the eight jobs together fits in one sentence: **understand a process, measure
it, and give someone the instrument to decide about it**. The scale changed and the tool changed;
the method is the same, and it has a name.

- **Time study and fatigue allowances**, at Inglopres: an observed time is not a standard until it
  acknowledges the effort of whoever performs it.
- **Line balancing and theory of constraints**, at Ceinfes: the bottleneck sets the real pace, and
  effective capacity —with exceptions and rework— is not the theoretical one.
- **Process modeling in BPMN and simulation**: Bizagi at Inglopres, Ceinfes, Cafam and Banco
  Pichincha, FlexSim at Inglopres and Cafam. Draw the process as executed before touching it, and
  test the alternative before deploying it.
- **An assignment problem with constraints**, at TransMilenio: driver, bus and route are not three
  decisions but one, and the prescriptive part begins by formulating it that way.
- **ISO 9001 and, today, ISO/IEC 42001**: the same high-level structure of ISO management
  systems, applied in 2016 to the quality of an operation and in 2025 to the management of
  artificial intelligence. What is done leaves a written trail, and that did not change.

That is why my career is not a departure from Industrial Engineering but its evolution: I went
from studying how to improve processes to building the analytical capabilities that make it
possible to understand them, and from analyzing decisions to building solutions that assist them.
An AI agent, seen this way, is not a feature: it is a new participant inside a work system, with
access to certain information, limits and a responsibility that has to be designed.

## How many years

<!-- seccion: cuantos-anos -->

My formal education is the undergraduate degree in Industrial Engineering from the Javeriana, with
an emphasis in Data Analytics Intelligence, and the Industrial Design program; I have no master's,
specialization or other graduate degree: the later deepening is the certifications and what I
have built. **Ten years of professional career**, counted from August 2016, and **eight with data
at the center**, counted from Ceinfes in November 2017, when indicators became my job and not a
tool of the job.

In that arc there are eight jobs in eight organizations and six sectors: heavy machinery,
educational assessment, mass transit, medicines logistics, banking and an AI agents startup;
today, the health sector. Added up, they are **105 months of effective work**, eight years and
nine months; the rest, up to the ten, are the pauses between one job and the next, the longest
between May 2022 and March 2023, which I devoted to studying: three of the four IBM
certifications —the 2022 ones— come from those months. The exact periods, month by month, are in
the table above.

If the question is how large my experience with AI is compared to that with data: data takes up
eight of those years; applied AI, with agents in production, starts in August 2023 at Vesting and
continues today at CTIC. Three years out of the ten, and the three most recent.
